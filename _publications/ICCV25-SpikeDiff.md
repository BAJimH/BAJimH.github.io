### Abstract

Spike camera continuously records intensity changes with high temporal resolution and high dynamic range, making it a promising alternative to frame-based cameras for high-speed imaging. Reconstructing high-quality color videos from chromatic spike streams remains challenging when only sub-millisecond spike streams are available without paired high-quality frames. We present SpikeDiff, a zero-shot method for high-quality video reconstruction from chromatic spike camera and sub-millisecond spike streams. By modeling the spike generation process and leveraging diffusion priors, SpikeDiff reconstructs temporally consistent, high-fidelity color videos without requiring task-specific paired training data. Extensive experiments on real and synthetic spike data demonstrate that SpikeDiff achieves superior reconstruction quality with reduced noise and motion blur under high-speed conditions.
